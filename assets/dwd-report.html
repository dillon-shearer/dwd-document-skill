DWD 1 - Summary

1. Summary

Twelve months of sales data show that repeat customers drive most of the growth. First-time buyers held steady, while people who had bought before spent more and came back sooner. The weekly detail is in section 4.

The store grew faster than the national e-commerce trend reported in the US Census Bureau quarterly retail survey, even before the second-half changes took effect.

  • Revenue rose in every quarter.
    • Q3 revenue was 22% above Q1.
    • Repeat customers supplied 61% of Q3 revenue.
  • Email brought in more revenue than any other channel.
  • Three changes explain most of the shift; section 2 lists them.
Note: All revenue figures are in thousands of US dollars before tax.

DWD 3 - Channels

3. Channels

Email led every other channel in Q3, and the repeat share climbed in every month of the year.

Revenue by channel

In Q3, email brought in $184k, search $140k, social $96k, direct $81k, referral $58k and partner sites $37k.

Repeat share by month

The repeat share rose from 44% in January to 63% in December, with the steepest climb between April and June.

DWD 5 - Method

5. Method

The analysis used the store's order export for 1 January to 31 December 2026. A customer counts as returning when the order_count field is above one.

Steps

  1. Export all orders and drop test and cancelled orders.
  2. Group orders by customer and count them.
  3. Sum revenue by segment, channel and week.

Query

The returning-customer revenue came from this query:

SELECT customer_id, COUNT(*) AS order_count, SUM(total) AS revenue
FROM orders
WHERE status = 'paid' AND order_date >= '2026-01-01'
GROUP BY customer_id
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;
